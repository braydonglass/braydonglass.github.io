01  How it works

Four plates share one camera and one crop. Because nothing shifts between them, the stack can cross-fade in place and read as a single fish opening up, rather than as four pictures taking turns. Registration is the whole trick; without it the illusion collapses immediately.

The section is a tall scroll runway with one viewport-sized frame pinned for its full length. Scrolling produces a single number between 0 and 1, and every layer derives its own opacity and offset from that one value, so the stack can never disagree with itself or with the captions.

The depth is real, and that is the whole point. Rotating a photograph in CSS only ever skews a flat card — there is no far side of the fish to bring round, so past a few degrees the trick shows. Instead every plate is sampled through a depth map in one fragment shader: each pixel's lookup is displaced by its own depth times the camera vector, so near pixels travel further across the frame than far ones. That is what parallax is, and it is why the flank now slides against the gravel behind it.

The offset is solved by fixed-point iteration rather than in a single step, so a near surface can occlude what sits behind it instead of smearing through it. The depth map itself was derived from the fish's own silhouette: a Euclidean distance transform of the outline, square-rooted into a rounded cross-section, which is close to how a trout is actually shaped through the body, over a gradient for the receding riverbed.

Because the shader carries the dimensionality, the camera only turns about seven degrees. It never needs to oversell. The nervous system is sampled on a deeper camera vector than the body, so it separates from the flesh it is threaded through as the frame moves rather than by being slid across it.

anime.js owns the camera. It is an createAnimatable(), handed target angles every frame and easing into them on its own clock; the shader then reads that eased angle straight back with utils.get() and turns it into the parallax vector, so the CSS rotation and the depth displacement can never come apart. animate() with stagger() brings this text in. Scroll position is measured off the runway directly rather than through onScroll(), whose sync scrubbing did not report reliably here.
