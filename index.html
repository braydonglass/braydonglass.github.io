01 / About

I want to know why it broke.

I graduated with a Computer Science degree from Commonwealth University-Mansfield. The part of the work I actually enjoy is the troubleshooting. Reproducing the problem, reading the whole error instead of skimming it, and changing one thing at a time until something gives.

My programming is mostly coursework and personal projects: Python, Java, C++, C#, and a good amount of SQL. The database work is what stuck. Designing a schema that holds up and writing a query that answers a real question is the part I keep going back to, and it's what both projects below are built around.

The hardware side I picked up by doing it. I've built four PCs from parts and kept them running since: picking components that actually work together, cable management, BIOS and driver setup, and the maintenance afterward when a fan starts dying or Windows decides to break itself. Diagnosing your own machine at 1am is good practice for diagnosing someone else's at 9am.

I've also been working customer-facing jobs since 2017: retail, food service, moving, sales. None of it was software. But it's where I learned to stay steady when someone's frustrated and explain a fix in plain language, and for help desk work that turns out to count for a lot.

Right now I'm looking for IT support work and building more projects on the side.

01

Inventory Management System

Complete

A SQL project modeling inventory for a multi-warehouse retail operation, with seven tables covering products, suppliers, warehouses, purchase orders, and stock movements. The part I'm happiest with: current stock isn't stored as a column, it's derived from a ledger of every transaction, so the numbers can't drift out of sync. Seventeen queries on top of it, using joins, CTEs, and window functions.

Complete

A Flask web app for searching adoptable cats by name, breed, size, and maximum age. Data gets seeded from a spreadsheet into SQLite, and the search endpoint builds the query from whichever filters you actually used instead of running one rigid query every time.
